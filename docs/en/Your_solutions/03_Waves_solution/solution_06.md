# 🌊 Wave Equation Problem – Step-by-Step Solution

A wave is described by the equation:

\[
y(x,t)=0.05\sin(2\pi x-50\pi t)
\]

where:

- \(x\) and \(y\) are in **meters**
- \(t\) is in **seconds**

We need to determine:

a) Amplitude \(A\)  
b) Wavelength \(\lambda\)  
c) Frequency \(f\)  
d) Wave speed \(v\)

---

# 📘 Step 1: Compare with the Standard Wave Equation

The standard form of a traveling wave is:

\[
y(x,t)=A\sin(kx-\omega t)
\]

where:

- \(A\) = amplitude  
- \(k\) = wave number  
- \(\omega\) = angular frequency  

---

# 📘 Given Equation

\[
y(x,t)=0.05\sin(2\pi x-50\pi t)
\]

Compare term by term:

- **Amplitude:** \(A=0.05\)
- **Wave number:** \(k=2\pi\)
- **Angular frequency:** \(\omega=50\pi\)

---

# ✅ a) Amplitude \(A\)

Amplitude is the coefficient in front of the sine function:

\[
A=0.05 \text{ m}
\]

### ✔ Final Answer:

\[
\boxed{A=0.05\text{ m}}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{5\text{ cm}}
\]

---

# ✅ b) Wavelength \(\lambda\)

Use the formula:

\[
k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}
\]

We know:

\[
k=2\pi
\]

Substitute:

\[
2\pi=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}
\]

Divide both sides by \(2\pi\):

\[
1=\frac{1}{\lambda}
\]

So:

\[
\lambda=1\text{ m}
\]

### ✔ Final Answer:

\[
\boxed{\lambda=1\text{ m}}
\]

---

# ✅ c) Frequency \(f\)

Use:

\[
\omega=2\pi f
\]

We know:

\[
\omega=50\pi
\]

Substitute:

\[
50\pi=2\pi f
\]

Divide both sides by \(2\pi\):

\[
f=25
\]

### ✔ Final Answer:

\[
\boxed{f=25\text{ Hz}}
\]

---

# ✅ d) Wave Speed \(v\)

Use:

\[
v=f\lambda
\]

Substitute values:

\[
v=25 \times 1
\]

\[
v=25\text{ m/s}
\]

### ✔ Final Answer:

\[
\boxed{v=25\text{ m/s}}
\]

---

# 📌 Final Answers Summary

| Quantity | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Amplitude \(A\) | \(0.05\text{ m}\) |
| Wavelength \(\lambda\) | \(1\text{ m}\) |
| Frequency \(f\) | \(25\text{ Hz}\) |
| Wave Speed \(v\) | \(25\text{ m/s}\) |

---

# 🧠 Extra Note

Because the equation has the form:

\[
\sin(kx-\omega t)
\]

the wave is traveling in the **positive x-direction**.

\[
\boxed{\text{Wave moves to the right}}
\]
