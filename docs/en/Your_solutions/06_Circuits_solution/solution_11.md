# Power and Energy in a Resistor

Given:

- Resistance:

$$
R = 100\Omega
$$

- Voltage:

$$
V = 50\text{ V}
$$

- Time:

$$
t = 5\text{ minutes}
$$

---

# Part 1: Find the Power Dissipated

## Step 1: Use the Power Formula

For a resistor:

$$
P = \frac{V^2}{R}
$$

---

## Step 2: Substitute Values

$$
P = \frac{50^2}{100}
$$

First calculate \(50^2\):

$$
50^2 = 2500
$$

Now substitute:

$$
P = \frac{2500}{100}
$$

---

## Step 3: Calculate

$$
P = 25
$$

---

# Power Answer

$$
\boxed{
P = 25\text{ W}
}
$$

---

# Part 2: Find the Energy Consumed in 5 Minutes

## Step 1: Convert Time to Seconds

$$
5\text{ minutes} = 5 \times 60
$$

$$
t = 300\text{ s}
$$

---

## Step 2: Use the Energy Formula

Energy:

$$
E = Pt
$$

---

## Step 3: Substitute Values

$$
E = 25 \times 300
$$

---

## Step 4: Calculate

$$
E = 7500
$$

---

# Final Energy Answer

$$
\boxed{
E = 7500\text{ J}
}
$$

---

# Final Answers

| Quantity | Result |
|---|---|
| Power dissipated | $$25\text{ W}$$ |
| Energy consumed in 5 minutes | $$7500\text{ J}$$ |

---

# Quick Explanation

- The resistor converts electrical energy into heat.
- Higher voltage produces more power.
- Energy depends on both power and time.

---
# Deriving the Formula

We want to understand where this formula comes from:

$$
P = \frac{V^2}{R}
$$

---

# Step 1: Start with the Basic Power Formula

Electrical power is defined as:

$$
P = VI
$$

where:

- \(P\) = power (watts)
- \(V\) = voltage (volts)
- \(I\) = current (amperes)

---

# Step 2: Use Ohm’s Law

Ohm’s Law says:

$$
V = IR
$$

We can solve for current:

$$
I = \frac{V}{R}
$$

---

# Step 3: Substitute Into the Power Formula

Start with:

$$
P = VI
$$

Replace \(I\) with:

$$
I = \frac{V}{R}
$$

So:

$$
P = V\left(\frac{V}{R}\right)
$$

---

# Step 4: Simplify

Multiply the voltages:

$$
P = \frac{V^2}{R}
$$

---

# Final Formula

$$
\boxed{
P = \frac{V^2}{R}
}
$$

---

# Other Equivalent Power Formulas

Starting from:

$$
P = VI
$$

and using Ohm’s Law, we can also get:

---

## Using \(V = IR\)

Substitute into power:

$$
P = I(IR)
$$

$$
P = I^2R
$$

---

# Final Set of Power Formulas

$$
\boxed{
P = VI
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
P = I^2R
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
P = \frac{V^2}{R}
}
$$

---

# Quick Intuition

- Large voltage → more power
- Large current → more power
- Resistance affects how much electrical energy becomes heat
